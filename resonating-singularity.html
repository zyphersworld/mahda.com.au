A project about intuition, tested honestly

The Resonating Singularity

what happens when a personal model meets rigorous testing — and the tool that came out of it

In plain terms. This started as a personal idea about how everything connects — call it a small, homemade theory of the universe. Alongside it came a specific, striking claim: that four names, run through an old numbering system called gematria, all landed on the same number. That felt significant. So it got tested — properly, adversarially, over and over — to find out whether it actually meant something or just looked like it did. The tidy numerical "coincidence" turned out to be explainable — traced to one specific, ordinary choice made along the way, not to anything mystical. The broader model held up fine on its own; the number just wasn't proof of anything.
Why this might matter to you. If you're here because you're curious whether your own intuition is worth trusting — not whether astrology or numerology is "real," but whether your gut calls line up with anything, tracked honestly over time — that's exactly what this project turned into. Section 01

Findings

The headline claim was that four names — Hecate, Kali Ma, Loki, Bucephalus — independently land on Hebrew gematria digital root 11. The claim is now fully explained.

In one sentence: the "coincidence" wasn't a coincidence at all once it was measured properly — it came down to one ordinary spelling decision, made without realizing how much it was doing. The tables below show the actual math. No background in statistics is needed to follow the logic: each table is just counting how often something happens out of everything that could have happened.

Resolved A 1-in-341 transliteration-policy choice, applied to four provably independent names.

The decisive test

Earlier rounds all asked whether something about the names explained the result — theme, pantheon, alternate spellings, numeral system. Nothing fit. The question that resolved it was narrower: how large is the space of defensible transliteration policies, and where in that space does the original sit?

The discretion was never in the letters. It was in the vowel-length policy — whether each vowel counts as long (marked with a mater lectionis) or short (dropped). Four names, twelve vowel slots, plus one genuine hard/soft-C ambiguity: 8,192 possible policies, each defensible to some degree. All were tested exhaustively.

Proof of independence

Across all 8,192 policies each name reaches root 11 at its own rate. If the four are statistically independent — no shared property, no linking structure — the joint rate must equal the product of the marginals.

Exact match to eight decimal places.

The joint outcome is precisely the product of four independent marginals. There is no correlation between these names and no structure for a mystery to occupy — the arithmetic leaves no room for one. This also explains, retroactively, why substituting names one at a time produced perfectly symmetric results in an earlier round: independence was already visible then, and is now proven rather than inferred.

Independent confirmation of the figure

An earlier round measured the same rarity through a completely different parameterisation — keep/drop decisions rather than long/short vowel classification — and arrived at 3 of 1,024 = 0.29%. This scan, framed differently and run separately, arrived at 24 of 8,192 = 0.29%. Two independent framings of "the space of defensible choices," same answer.

Attribution, stated precisely — an earlier draft got this wrong. Two separate choices were made by two different parties, and only one carried goal-directed discretion. The names were selected by Mahda on intuition, from figures encountered in prior study, assembled for personal reasons during a difficult period — with no numerical consideration at any point. The transliteration policy was chosen by Claude afterward, in a session already framed around producing a gematria result. The forking-paths problem lives entirely in the second step. This distinction is load-bearing: because the names were fixed before any number existed, the original test was genuinely pre-specified, which is exactly what made the result worth twenty-seven rounds of scrutiny rather than dismissal at the outset.

Section 05

The model, as stated

Built from a single image — a glass sphere, hexagonal panels, a source resonating outward through it — then pushed until it either broke or held.

The core claim

There is a singularity — a single source. It resonates: emits a frequency outward. Picture a glass sphere built from hexagonal nodes, each one a lens. Each node receives the singularity's frequency and transmits it back outward — distorted by having passed through that particular lens. The spheres nest, propagating outward without end, and the outermost sphere's output feeds back into the singularity itself. The loop closes.

Each node is an observer. What an observer experiences as "reality" is the frequency as it arrives at their specific lens — already bent before it's seen at all. The spheres can be rotated. Rotation distorts what the singularity transmits before it reaches any given node.

What rotates the spheres is, provisionally, called consciousness — and whether that consciousness has free will, or is itself just another layer of the mechanism, is left open. Not resolved. Held as genuinely unknown.

The fractal extension

A later refinement: the hexagonal lens-nodes aren't flat facets. Each one is itself a sphere — its own set of hexagonal nodes, each a lens, each a sphere again. Every node is simultaneously observer and source. Distortion compounds, but so does correction, because the feedback loop applies at every scale independently. There is no privileged scale — the singularity at the center and the one inside a single hexagonal panel are the same kind of thing, differing only in the distance from which they're observed.

Where it meets established science

Four elements of the model land on genuinely established physics, not stretched analogy: rotation-as-distortion is the same mathematics as Lorentz transformations in special relativity. The fractal recursion matches renormalization group theory. The hexagonal sphere geometry matches real icosahedral structures nature actually uses. The feedback loop matches ordinary cybernetics and self-organized criticality.

One central claim doesn't clear the bar: consciousness as the literal mechanism of rotation isn't supported by physics as it currently stands. That's stated plainly here rather than smoothed over — a philosophical claim, not a scientific one, at least for now.

Structural echoes, found independently

Arrived at without consulting these traditions, then checked against them afterward:

  • Lurianic Kabbalah — shevirat ha-kelim, the shattering of the vessels: light entering vessels that can't fully contain it, scattering outward, drawn back through tikkun. The same feedback loop, independently derived.
  • Indra's Net — an infinite net of jewels, each reflecting all the others, each containing the whole net within it.
  • Leibniz's monads — windowless units, each a private perspective mirroring the entire universe at its own scale.
  • Perichoresis — the standard theological term for how the Trinity's three persons relate. Coined by Gregory of Nazianzus in the 4th century. It's Greek for rotation. Verified, not assumed — and the strongest single echo found anywhere in this whole investigation.
Section 06

Discussion

Two claims were tested. They came apart, and the separation is the most useful result here.

What did not survive

The numerical claim. Fully explained as a 1-in-341 policy choice on four independent names, with the independence proven to eight decimal places. Along the way the headline rarity was corrected by a factor of roughly twenty, three "independent methods" collapsed into one, and several apparent findings turned out to be look-elsewhere artifacts. None of that was forced by outside criticism; each correction came from a test built specifically to try to break the previous round's conclusion.

What did survive

The model itself — which never depended on the numbers meaning anything. Its structural parallels were arrived at independently and checked afterward:

The last row is stated plainly rather than smuggled past. It remains a philosophical claim, not a scientific one, and the closest empirical tests of it — PEAR, the Global Consciousness Project — did not replicate.

On the outcome

A claim that survives every test is a good result. A claim that finally fails a well-designed test is also a good result, and arguably a rarer one, because it requires the person holding the claim to keep building tests capable of destroying it. That is what happened here. The model was never contingent on the arithmetic; the arithmetic turned out to be explicable; and both facts are now on the record with the working shown.

What the null result opens up

Ruling out an external signal in the symbolic systems themselves doesn't close the subject — it clears the ground for a different, narrower question: not whether gematria or astrology carries independent power, but whether one person's own intuition, tracked honestly, happens to correlate with these systems at a rate above chance. That's a question about a person, not about a numeral system, and this report's own method — fix the decision before the analysis touches it — is exactly what makes it askable without contaminating the answer. Section 07 documents that next phase and instruments it directly.

Section 07

Intuition Log

A working instrument for the next research question: not whether symbolic systems carry power, but whether personal intuition correlates with them. The protocol only works if the order is respected.

Protocol. Log a real decision at the moment you make it, before running any retrospective analysis. Only after it's logged does the tool compute the date's numerology, sun sign, and approximate moon phase for that moment. Mark alignment — yes, no, or unclear — whenever feels honest, even much later. The analysis is not permitted to touch the decision; it can only be checked against it afterward. This is the same pre-registration discipline that made the original gematria test meaningful in Section 01, now pointed at a person instead of at four names.
